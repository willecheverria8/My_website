Teaching Experience

I believe we can only teach what we can question and critique in a deep and rigorous way. My experience shows the importance of keeping students’ minds open and helping them engage critically with evidence, theory, and policy. I have designed course curricula and co-led the design of a full bachelor’s program in Economics, as well as updated undergraduate curricula in Business and Economics.

My pedagogy is guided by a simple cycle: begin with a question, examine the evidence, use theory, models, and analytical tools to answer or refine the question, and then identify the limits of those answers to open new questions.

Economic Sociology

Teaching evaluations: 9.27

April 2025 - August 2025

For first-year Economics students. Economic sociology examines how material and symbolic conditions of life are produced and reproduced through social processes. The course brings together sociology and economics to understand how economic life is embedded in social relations, focusing on two core concepts: markets and embeddedness. It compares economic and sociological views of markets, explores how societies shape markets and how societies, in turn, shape them, and applies these ideas to real-world cases through contemporary examples.
